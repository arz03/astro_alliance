Comprehensive Guide to the Variables in the Exoplanet Dataset

1. pl_rade (Planet Radius [Earth Radii] Target Variable 1) = The radius of the exoplanet
expressed as a multiple of Earth's radius.

2. pl_bmasse (Planet Best Mass [Earth Masses] Target Variable 2) = The best estimate of the
mass of the planet, measured in units of Earth masses. This is typically the measured value (M) or
minimum mass (M sin i) depending on the discovery technique.

3. pl_orbper (Orbital Period [Days]) = The amount of time it takes for the exoplanet to
complete one full orbit around its host star, measured in solar days.

4. pl_orbpererr1 (Orbital Period Upper Uncertainty) = The positive (upper) margin of error or
measurement uncertainty for the planet's orbital period.

5. pl_orbpererr2 (Orbital Period Lower Uncertainty) = The negative (lower) margin of error
or measurement uncertainty for the planet's orbital period.

6. pl_orbsmax (Orbit Semi-Major Axis [AU]) = The semi-major axis of the planet's elliptical
orbit, which represents the average distance from the host star in Astronomical Units (AU).

7. pl_orbsmaxerr1 (Orbit Semi-Major Axis Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of error
for the planet's semi-major axis measurement.

8. pl_orbsmaxerr2 (Orbit Semi-Major Axis Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of
error for the planet's semi-major axis measurement.

9. pl_orbeccen (Eccentricity) = The amount by which the planet's orbit deviates from a perfect
circle (0 = perfect circle, values closer to 1 signify highly elongated orbits).

10. pl_orbincl (Orbital Inclination [Deg]) = The angle of the planet's orbital plane relative to the
plane of the sky or the reference line from Earth, measured in degrees.

11. pl_insol (Insolation Flux [Earth Flux]) = The amount of stellar energy received by the planet
per unit area compared to the energy Earth receives from the Sun.

12. pl_eqt (Equilibrium Temperature [K]) = The theoretical temperature calculated for the
planet assuming it acts as a blackbody heated solely by its host star, measured in Kelvin.

13. pl_imppar (Impact Parameter) = The minimum distance between the center of the stellar
disk and the center of the planet's trajectory during a transit event, normalized by stellar radius.

14. pl_trandur (Transit Duration [Hours]) = The duration of time it takes for a transiting planet
to pass completely across the face of its host star, measured in hours.

15. pl_ratror (Ratio of Planet Radius to Stellar Radius) = The dimensionless size ratio of the
planet's radius divided by its host star's radius. Essential for transit depth analysis.

16. pl_ratrorerr1 (Ratio of Planet Radius to Stellar Radius Upper Uncertainty) = The
positive margin of error for the radius ratio.

17. pl_ratrorerr2 (Ratio of Planet Radius to Stellar Radius Lower Uncertainty) = The
negative margin of error for the radius ratio.

18. st_mass (Stellar Mass [Solar Masses]) = The mass of the host star expressed as a multiple of
our Sun's mass.

19. st_masserr1 (Stellar Mass Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of error for the host
star's mass.

20. st_masserr2 (Stellar Mass Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of error for the host
star's mass.

21. st_rad (Stellar Radius [Solar Radii]) = The radius of the host star expressed as a multiple of
our Sun's radius.

22. st_raderr1 (Stellar Radius Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of error for the host
star's radius.

23. st_raderr2 (Stellar Radius Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of error for the host
star's radius.

24. st_teff (Stellar Effective Temperature [K]) = The surface temperature of the host star
determined from its spectral characteristics, measured in Kelvin.

25. st_tefferr1 (Stellar Effective Temperature Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of
error for the stellar surface temperature.

26. st_tefferr2 (Stellar Effective Temperature Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of
error for the stellar surface temperature.

27. st_logg (Stellar Surface Gravity [log10(cm/s²)]) = The base-10 logarithm of the gravitational
acceleration at the surface of the host star.

28. st_loggerr1 (Stellar Surface Gravity Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of error for
the stellar surface gravity.

29. st_loggerr2 (Stellar Surface Gravity Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of error
for the stellar surface gravity.


30. st_met (Stellar Metallicity [dex]) = The abundance of elements heavier than hydrogen and
helium present in the star, relative to the Sun's metallicity (expressed on a logarithmic scale).

31. st_meterr1 (Stellar Metallicity Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of error for the
stellar metallicity.

32. st_meterr2 (Stellar Metallicity Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of error for the
stellar metallicity.

33. st_dens (Stellar Density [g/cm³]) = The average density of the host star, calculated in grams
per cubic centimeter.

34. sy_vmag (System V-band Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary system
measured through a standard visual (V) optical filter. Lower numbers mean brighter objects.

35. sy_vmagerr1 (System V-band Magnitude Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of
error for the system's V-band magnitude.

36. sy_vmagerr2 (System V-band Magnitude Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of
error for the system's V-band magnitude.

37. sy_bmag (System B-band Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary system in
the blue (B) optical filter spectrum.

38. sy_bmagerr1 (System B-band Magnitude Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of
error for the system's B-band magnitude.

39. sy_bmagerr2 (System B-band Magnitude Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of
error for the system's B-band magnitude.

40. sy_jmag (System J-band Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary system
measured in the near-infrared J-band wavelength (~1.25 micrometers).

41. sy_jmagerr1 (System J-band Magnitude Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of
error for the system's J-band magnitude.

42. sy_jmagerr2 (System J-band Magnitude Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of
error for the system's J-band magnitude.

43. sy_hmag (System H-band Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary system in
the near-infrared H-band wavelength (~1.65 micrometers).

44. sy_hmagerr1 (System H-band Magnitude Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of
error for the system's H-band magnitude.

45. sy_hmagerr2 (System H-band Magnitude Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of
error for the system's H-band magnitude.

46. sy_kmag (System K-band Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary system in
the near-infrared K-band wavelength (~2.2 micrometers).


47. sy_kmagerr1 (System K-band Magnitude Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of
error for the system's K-band magnitude.

48. sy_kmagerr2 (System K-band Magnitude Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of
error for the system's K-band magnitude.

49. sy_w1mag (System WISE W1 Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary
system in the mid-infrared W1 band (~3.4 micrometers) measured by the Wide-field Infrared
Survey Explorer.

50. sy_w1magerr1 (System WISE W1 Magnitude Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin
of error for the W1 magnitude.

51. sy_w1magerr2 (System WISE W1 Magnitude Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin
of error for the W1 magnitude.

52. sy_w2mag (System WISE W2 Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary
system in the mid-infrared W2 band (~4.6 micrometers) from the WISE telescope.

53. sy_w2magerr1 (System WISE W2 Magnitude Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin
of error for the W2 magnitude.

54. sy_w2magerr2 (System WISE W2 Magnitude Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin
of error for the W2 magnitude.

55. sy_w3mag (System WISE W3 Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary
system in the thermal-infrared W3 band (~12 micrometers) from the WISE telescope.

56. sy_w3magerr1 (System WISE W3 Magnitude Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin
of error for the W3 magnitude.

57. sy_w3magerr2 (System WISE W3 Magnitude Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin
of error for the W3 magnitude.

58. sy_w4mag (System WISE W4 Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the planetary
system in the far-infrared W4 band (~22 micrometers) from the WISE telescope.

59. sy_gaiamag (System Gaia Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the system as measured
across the broad optical band by the European Space Agency's Gaia spacecraft.

60. sy_tmag (System TESS Magnitude) = The apparent brightness of the system in the
specialized broad red/near-infrared band used by NASA's Transiting Exoplanet Survey Satellite.

61. sy_snum (Number of Stars in System) = The total number of cataloged stars belonging to
this particular planetary system (e.g., binary, tertiary, or single star configurations).

62. sy_pnum (Number of Planets in System) = The total number of confirmed exoplanets
discovered orbiting within this planetary system.

63. sy_mnum (Number of Moons in System) = The total number of confirmed or candidate
natural satellites (exomoons) discovered around the exoplanets in this system.


64. discoverymethod (Discovery Method) = The astronomical technique used to originally
detect and confirm the exoplanet (e.g., Transit, Radial Velocity, Direct Imaging, Microlensing).

65. disc_year (Discovery Year) = The calendar year in which the exoplanet's discovery was
officially confirmed and published in scientific literature.

66. disc_facility (Discovery Facility) = The specific observatory, ground station, or space-based
telescope mission responsible for detecting the exoplanet (e.g., Kepler, TESS, W. M. Keck
Observatory).

67. soltype (Solution Type) = The cataloging tag designating the nature of the data entry solution
or the processing pipeline settings applied to the planetary record.

68. sy_dist (System Distance [Parsecs]) = The distance from Earth to the planetary system,
measured in units of parsecs (1 parsec ≈ 3.26 light-years).

69. sy_disterr1 (System Distance Upper Uncertainty) = The positive margin of error for the
distance parameter.

70. sy_disterr2 (System Distance Lower Uncertainty) = The negative margin of error for the
distance parameter.

71. sy_plx (System Parallax [mas]) = The stellar parallax angle measured in milliarcseconds,
which forms the trigonometric basis for calculating interstellar distance from Earth.

72. sy_pm (System Total Proper Motion [mas/yr]) = The overall angular velocity of the system
across the sky relative to distant background stars, measured in milliarcseconds per year.

73. sy_pmra (System Proper Motion (RA) [mas/yr]) = The component of the system's proper
motion along the direction of Right Ascension.

74. sy_pmdec (System Proper Motion (Dec) [mas/yr]) = The component of the system's proper
motion along the direction of Declination.

75. glon (Galactic Longitude [Deg]) = The angular coordinate of the planetary system measured
eastward along the galactic equator from the Galactic Center, in degrees.

76. glat (Galactic Latitude [Deg]) = The angular distance of the planetary system north or south
of the plane of the Milky Way galaxy, in degrees.

77. cosmic_noise_1 (Cosmic Noise Filter Feature 1) = A synthesized or simulated noise
threshold used in the analytics pipeline to account for stellar activity or instrument artifacts.

78. cosmic_noise_2 (Cosmic Noise Filter Feature 2) = A secondary diagnostic noise variable
used for regularizing or validating model features.

79. pl_orbper_noisy (Noisy Orbital Period) = A perturbed or synthetically modified version of
the orbital period feature, typically utilized for training robustness testing or data augmentations.

80. pl_orbsmax_noisy (Noisy Semi-Major Axis) = A perturbed or artificially altered version of
the semi-major axis feature, used to benchmark analytical models against feature variation.


81. orbital_size_score (Orbital Size Score) = A calculated engineered score reflecting the
geometric scale of the orbit relative to standard reference systems.

82. stellar_scale_score (Stellar Scale Score) = An engineered metric summarizing the
dimensions and mass traits of the host star for simplified multi-variable grouping.

83. rowid (Row Identifier) = The unique internal integer index mapping each row record in this
data matrix.

84. pl_name (Planet Name) = The official systematic designation given to the exoplanet (usually
consisting of the host star name followed by a lowercase letter starting with 'b').

85. hostname (Host Star Name) = The primary scientific name of the parent star or multiple-star
system around which the exoplanet orbits.

86. pl_letter (Planet Letter Designation) = The lowercase letter assigned to the exoplanet in
order of discovery within its system (e.g., b, c, d).

87. rowupdate (Row Last Update Timestamp) = The calendar date when this planetary entry
was last updated or verified in the data archive database.

88. pl_pubdate (Planet Parameters Publication Date) = The year and month corresponding to
the primary scientific paper publication from which these planet properties were extracted.

89. releasedate (Data Release Date) = The administrative date when this specific entry row was
made publicly available in the integrated analytics dataset.

90. disc_pubdate (Discovery Publication Date) = The scientific publication date tracking the
original research article that declared the formal confirmation of the planet.

91. st_nphot (Number of Photometric Observations) = The total count of distinct light curve or
photometric measurement sequences available for the host star in public.

92. st_nrvc (Number of Radial Velocity Observations) = The total number of high-precision
radial velocity or Doppler shift spectra cataloged for this host star.

93. st_nspec (Number of Stellar Spectra Books) = The total number of atmospheric or chemical
spectral profiles captured and archived for the parent star.

94. pl_nespec (Number of Emission Spectra Datasets) = The count of atmospheric emission
spectra datasets collected for the planet during secondary eclipses or phase changes.

95. pl_ntranspec (Number of Transmission Spectra Datasets) = The count of atmospheric
transmission spectra records obtained during transits to probe the chemical composition of the
exoplanet's limbs.

96. pl_ndispec (Number of Direct Imaging Spectra) = The total number of direct imaging or
coronagraphic spectroscopic files resolved for this exoplanet.
